## Pricing Terms

Pharmaceutical supply contracts should define a clear pricing structure with the following
elements:

1. **Base price**: A fixed unit price at contract inception.
2. **Adjustment cap**: A maximum percentage by which prices may be adjusted annually,
   typically tied to a published index such as the Consumer Price Index (CPI).
3. **Review process**: A formal process with advance notice (recommended 60–90 days)
   before any price adjustment takes effect.
4. **Consent requirement**: Any adjustment above the defined cap must require written
   mutual consent.

Vague pricing adjustment language without a defined cap or structured review process
materially increases financial dispute risk. Language such as "prices may change based
on market conditions" without qualification is considered a high-risk contract term.

## Liability and Indemnification

Pharma contracts should define explicit liability caps, exclusions, and a structured
review process to protect both parties. Key elements include:

- **Liability cap**: A maximum aggregate liability expressed as a multiple of contract
  value or a fixed monetary limit.
- **Exclusions**: Clear carve-outs for gross negligence, fraud, and willful misconduct.
- **Consequential damages**: A clear position on whether indirect damages are excluded.
- **Indemnification scope**: Defined obligations for each party in product liability,
  regulatory action, and third-party claims scenarios.

Contracts with no liability cap, or with language expressly removing any limitation,
are considered high-risk and should be escalated to legal counsel before execution.

## Termination Terms

Termination clauses should include:

- **Termination for cause**: Defined triggers and cure periods (typically 30 days).
- **Termination for convenience**: A minimum notice period of 90 days to protect
  supply continuity and allow transition planning.
- **Wind-down obligations**: Commitments to fulfill outstanding orders during any
  notice period.
- **Transition assistance**: Supply bridge obligations when a contract ends to prevent
  patient access gaps.

Contracts permitting immediate termination without cause or without an adequate notice
period present operational and patient care risks.

## Audit Rights

Pharmaceutical supply contracts should include explicit audit rights:

- Annual audit right with 30-day advance notice.
- Extraordinary audit right within 5–10 business days for safety or compliance events.
- Defined scope covering quality records, batch documentation, and regulatory compliance.
- Remediation obligations following audit findings.

---

## Contract Structure Best Practices

1. All material obligations should be explicitly defined with no ambiguous open-ended language.
2. Patient impact should be a stated consideration in deviation reporting, delivery, and
   termination clauses.
3. Both parties should have defined escalation pathways for disputes and supply issues.
4. Contracts should be reviewed by qualified legal and compliance professionals before execution.
