## Supply Continuity and Escalation

Procurement contracts must define delivery timelines, escalation paths, and contingency
obligations when primary supply is unavailable. Key requirements include:

1. **Maximum delivery lead time**: A defined number of calendar days from order confirmation
   to delivery. For critical medications, lead times should not exceed 14 calendar days
   under normal operating conditions.

2. **Supply disruption notification**: Suppliers must notify the procurement organization
   within 48 hours of any anticipated supply disruption.

3. **Escalation procedure**: If a delivery delay exceeds five business days, the supplier
   must provide a written remediation plan including alternative sourcing options.

4. **Patient-impacting supply issues require escalation**: Any supply disruption that may
   affect patient access to treatment must be communicated to clinical leadership within
   24 hours.

Contracts conditioned on availability without a defined escalation timeline or substitution
process create unacceptable supply continuity risk for patient-facing procurement programs.

## Delivery Terms

Delivery terms should be specific and non-ambiguous:

- Delivery dates must be expressed in defined business or calendar days, not as
  "subject to availability" or "at supplier's discretion."
- Penalties or remedies for chronic delivery failure must be specified.
- Force majeure events do not absolve the supplier of all notification obligations;
  timely notification is required regardless of cause.

---

## Contract Termination

Healthcare procurement contracts should require adequate notice periods for termination
to protect supply continuity and patient care. Recommended minimum:

- **Termination for convenience**: 90 days prior written notice.
- **Termination for cause**: 30-day cure period before termination is effective.
- During any termination notice period, supply obligations continue in full.

Immediate or convenience termination without a specified notice period is a high-risk
contract term in a patient-care procurement context.

## Vendor Qualification

Procurement organizations should ensure that pharmaceutical suppliers:

1. Hold all required manufacturing and distribution licenses.
2. Maintain current GMP certification or equivalent quality standard.
3. Have demonstrated supply continuity history for critical medications.
4. Have defined internal escalation procedures for quality and compliance events.

---

## Patient Care Obligations

Healthcare procurement contracts should explicitly acknowledge that the products
procured may affect patient health outcomes. This acknowledgment should appear in
the contract scope and inform:

- Deviation reporting timelines (shorter windows for patient-impacting products).
- Escalation procedures (faster escalation when treatment continuity is at risk).
- Termination protections (longer notice periods to prevent treatment disruption).

When a supply issue, quality failure, or contract termination may affect patient access
to treatment, the contracting organization has an obligation to ensure continuity planning
is in place before any disruption occurs.

## Compliance Documentation

Procurement contracts should require:

- Current certificates of analysis for each delivered batch.
- Temperature and humidity monitoring records for temperature-sensitive products.
- Chain of custody documentation for all shipments.
- Prompt disclosure of any recall, regulatory action, or safety communication related
  to contracted products.
