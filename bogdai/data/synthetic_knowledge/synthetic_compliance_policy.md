## Deviation Reporting

Material deviations should be escalated promptly when they may affect product quality,
availability, or patient safety. The synthetic standard for material deviation reporting
is a maximum of thirty (30) days from the date of discovery.

For deviations that may immediately affect patient safety or treatment continuity, prompt
notification within twenty-four (24) to forty-eight (48) hours is expected. Delays in
deviation reporting increase regulatory exposure, audit risk, and remediation cost.

Deviations that go unreported or are reported beyond the thirty-day window are considered
a significant compliance gap that may result in regulatory escalation or audit findings.

## Audit and Oversight

Contracts with regulated suppliers should include defined audit rights with clear scope,
frequency, and escalation procedures. Annual audits of manufacturing, quality, and
compliance records are expected at a minimum for pharmaceutical supply partners.

Extraordinary audit rights should be available within five to ten business days when a
regulatory action, patient safety event, or material deviation has been identified.

Absence of audit rights in a pharmaceutical supply contract is a compliance risk indicator.

---

## Quality Management Systems

Pharmaceutical suppliers are expected to maintain a documented Quality Management System
(QMS) aligned with current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) standards. Key elements
include batch record review, deviation management, change control, and supplier qualification.

Failure to maintain cGMP compliance is grounds for contract review and potential supply
suspension under this synthetic policy standard.

---

## Regulatory Non-Conformance

Regulatory non-conformance events must be disclosed to purchasing organizations within
the deviation reporting window. Concealment of regulatory non-conformance is considered
a material breach of the synthetic policy standard.

---

## Patient Safety Obligations

When a supply issue, quality failure, or compliance event may affect patient access to
treatment, suppliers have an obligation to escalate through defined channels immediately.
Supply continuity plans must account for patient impact in the event of any disruption.
